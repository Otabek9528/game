    0

    Get ready

    15 minutes

    Collect these before you start, so no form stops you halfway.

    1. A Google account that will own the app.

      Make a new Gmail just for this, for example muslimvegukin.app@gmail.com, so the app doesn't depend on your personal mailbox. Turn on 2-Step Verification for it (Google account → Security). Use this one account for everything below.

    2. A bank card that works online, for the one-time registration fee of US$25. Korean cards (Visa, Mastercard) work.
    3. Your passport or Korean residence card (ARC).

      Google checks your identity. The name you type must match the document exactly.

    4. Your Android phone.

      Google asks new developers to prove they own a real Android phone, and you'll test the app on it.

    5. Start asking people to test.

      Google requires a test with at least 12 people for 14 days before the app can go public (phase 8). They need an Android phone and a Gmail. Ask 15–20 people in your Telegram group, so you still have 12 if someone drops out. The message to send is in phase 8.1; you can send it today.

    Create your developer account

    30 minutesa few hours to a few days

    This is your publisher account on Google Play. You create it once; all your apps live in it.

    play.google.com/console/signup
    1. Open play.google.com/console/signup and sign in with the Google account from phase 0.
    2. Choose an account type → Yourself.

      "An organization or business" needs a registered company and a D-U-N-S number. Choose it only if you have a company.

    3. Fill in the forms:
      FieldWhat to enter
      Developer nameShown on Google Play under the app's name. Muslim Vegukin (or your own name). You can change it later.
      Legal name and addressExactly as on your passport or ARC; your address in Korea. Google compares them with the document.
      Contact email and phonePrivate, for Google to reach you. They send codes to check both.
      Developer email (public)Shown on the store page so users can write to you. Use the new Gmail.
      Questions about youExperience, how many apps you plan, whether you'll earn money from them. Answer honestly (e.g. less than 1 year, 1 app, no earnings); they don't decide approval.
    4. Accept the Developer Distribution Agreement and pay the US$25.
    5. Identity check. Upload clear photos of your passport or ARC, the whole document, nothing cut off. The answer comes by email, from a few hours to several days.
    6. Android phone check. On your Android phone, install Google Play Console from the Play Store, sign in with the same Google account, and follow its verification prompt.
    7. Open play.google.com/console. When the page shows no red Action needed items, the account is ready.

    Watch out. Never make a second developer account: Google can close both. If something goes wrong in this phase, tell me before trying again.

    3

    Build the app file (.aab)

    10 minutes

    Google doesn't take the APK you shared with your friend. It wants an app bundle (.aab), signed with your key, and makes the phone-specific APKs from it itself.

    1. Check the version. Open E:\dev\game\mobile\pubspec.yaml and find version: 1.0.0+1.

      1.0.0 is what users see. The number after + is the version code: each file you upload to Google must have a bigger one than the last. Keep +1 for the first upload; for every later upload raise it (1.0.1+2, 1.0.2+3…).

    2. In PowerShell, run these one at a time. Paste each line whole (last time a long line got cut off).
      PowerShell
      cd E:\dev\game
git pull origin claude/intelligent-clarke-88a75i
cd mobile
flutter clean
flutter pub get
flutter build appbundle --release --obfuscate --split-debug-info=build/symbols
    3. The result: E:\dev\game\mobile\build\app\outputs\bundle\release\app-release.aab. This is the file you'll upload.
    4. Copy the folder E:\dev\game\mobile\build\symbols somewhere safe and name it after the version (e.g. symbols-1.0.0+1). It turns crash reports back into readable ones later.

    Watch out. Keep android\key.properties in place: without it the bundle is signed with the debug key and Google refuses it. Your .jks file and its password must never be lost: every future update has to be signed with the same key.

    Words you'll see

    Play Console
    Google's website for publishers, play.google.com/console. Google Play (the Play Store) is the shop people install from.
    App bundle (.aab)
    The file you upload. Google makes the APKs phones install from it. APKs are only for sharing directly, as you did with your friend.
    Track
    Who a version goes to: Internal testing (up to 100 people, instant, no review), Closed testing (invited people), Production (everyone).
    Release
    One version put on one track. The same bundle can be released on several tracks.
    Rollout
    Making a release available. A staged rollout reaches a percentage of users first.
    Review
    Google checks releases before people get them (all tracks except internal testing).
    Version code
    The number after + in pubspec.yaml. Must grow with every upload. The version name (1.0.0) is what users see.
    Upload key
    Your .jks. You sign bundles with it; Google checks uploads really come from you.
    App signing key
    Google's own key, which signs the app phones install. Its SHA-1 goes into Firebase for Google sign-in (phase 7).
    Opt-in link
    The link testers open to join a test before they can install it.
